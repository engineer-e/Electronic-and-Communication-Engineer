## **Chapter 1: A question nobody answered**

There was once a student who wanted to understand how the world worked.

He was fascinated by mathematics, physics, computers, engineering and science. He did not want merely to pass examinations. He wanted to discover something that nobody had discovered before.

He wanted to build machines, understand nature, develop algorithms, study biological systems and contribute to research.

He entered an engineering college believing that this was where his journey would begin.

He imagined laboratories full of experiments, professors discussing unsolved problems, research groups working together and students building things that might change the future.

But reality was different.

There were classrooms. There were examinations. There were assignments. There were marks.

Yet when he asked:

"How do I become a research scientist?"

There was no clear answer.

"How do I find a research mentor?"

Nobody guided him.

"How do I turn a mathematical idea into a research project?"

He did not know whom to ask.

"How do I publish my first scientific paper?"

The path was invisible.

The student had entered an engineering college, but he could not find the research environment he had imagined.

## **The next generation should have a better system**

I would build the research around five connected areas:

**1. Education accountability**

Who is responsible for delivering quality education?

**2. Research funding accountability**

Where does the funding go, and who receives the benefit?

**3. Human potential and lost opportunities**

How many people face barriers to developing their capabilities?

**4. Institutional comparison**

What practices make research-intensive universities different, and which can be implemented elsewhere?

**5. A practical alternative**

How can we create affordable, mentor-supported research pathways for students from ordinary colleges?

**One principle I would never compromise:** We should not claim that every unsuccessful student would have become a great scientist, or that every college is exploiting students. We should establish what opportunities were promised, what was actually provided, what outcomes followed, and which failures can be demonstrated.

The purpose is not to make future generations hate education or universities.

It is to make education worthy of the trust that families place in it.
